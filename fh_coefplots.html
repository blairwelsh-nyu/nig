Farmer–Herder Conflict and Women's Access to Health Services:
Evidence from Nigeria's Middle Belt

Working Paper  ·  Nigeria DHS 2003–2018  ·  October 2026

Abstract

We estimate the causal effect of farmer–herder conflict on women's healthcare utilization in Nigeria's Middle Belt. Using a four-wave difference-in-differences design (2003, 2008, 2013, 2018) that exploits variation in post-2009 ACLED conflict intensity across Local Government Areas, we find that women with a recent birth in high-conflict LGAs experienced declines in skilled birth attendance (−16.2 percentage points on the birth subsample, N = 21,517; −9.4 pp controlling for education composition). A four-wave event study using the 2003 DHS as a pre-treatment wave finds no evidence against parallel pre-trends. Positive mechanism tests point to supply-side disruption: conflict increases financial access barriers (+8.3 pp, p = 0.002) while distance barriers are not significantly affected; under a mutually exclusive highest-qualified-attendant coding, births shift from nurse/midwife (−12.1 pp**) toward TBA (+16.2 pp**) and unattended home birth (+8.5 pp**), with the skilled and traditional categories summing to zero by construction; DPT3 vaccination coverage declines marginally (−11.4 pp, p = 0.063); and a dose-response gradient is significant per log unit of conflict exposure. The Fulani–non-Fulani interaction is not significant (p > 0.6); religion heterogeneity is imprecisely estimated and should not be read as establishing targeted exclusion or its absence. Identification concerns: adding state × survey-year fixed effects attenuates the within-state estimate to −7.2 pp (p = 0.089†); combining state × year FE with an education control yields −4.7 pp (p = 0.147, ns); the within-state specification is underpowered (MDE = 11.7 pp at 80% power). Education compositional change is inflationary: conflict-wave women are lower-educated (b = −0.36, p = 0.011*), biasing the raw estimate upward. A within-mother fixed-effects design using the DHS birth history (the strongest available identification strategy) finds a near-zero effect on skilled delivery in all specifications: b = +0.31 pp (SE = 0.81, p = 0.703) with pre-2013 treatment, b = −0.01 pp (p = 0.991) for the 2018 Benue-escalation window, and pooled b = +0.09 pp (p = 0.950; N = 2,777 straddler mothers, 6,247 births). Birth order control does not change this (post-2009 births are higher parity but birth order does not predict skilled delivery within mothers). This null under the most demanding design substantially qualifies the DiD estimates. Rambachan–Roth sensitivity analysis (4-wave design, pre-trend b = −0.052, p = 0.18) finds the main estimates are not robust to even modest violations of parallel trends (M̅ = 0.5: CI includes zero for 2013 and 2018). The evidence is directionally consistent but fragile under demanding identification. A Lancet GH 50 km specification (Amberg et al. 2023) replication finds consistent effects (−5.3 pp*, p = 0.011), and a Callaway–Sant’Anna staggered DiD shows flat pre-trends with an underpowered average ATT (−3.6 pp, ns).

DHS sampling weights
v005 survey weights; weighted vs. unweighted DiD		 The DHS IR file includes sampling weights (v005) that adjust for unequal sampling probabilities across states and strata. Unweighted and weighted marginal means are very similar (skilled delivery 21.89% vs. 21.11%; ANC 58.49% vs. 57.04%; no attendant 17.21% vs. 18.40%; all <1.5 pp). However, applying WLS weights to the DiD estimator is more consequential: weighted b = −8.65 pp (p = 0.080†) vs. unweighted −16.2 pp** — 47% attenuation. The 47% attenuation warrants a deeper investigation. A cluster-level check reveals that only ~20% of 2013 MB clusters are within 5km of a 2008 cluster (median nearest-2008-cluster distance = 13.1 km for 2013 clusters), meaning the DHS effectively drew an independent random sample of clusters in each wave — not a panel. Within-LGA demeaning removes the LGA average but does not control for which sub-areas within an LGA happened to be sampled in each wave. If treated LGAs systematically shifted toward lower-SES sub-areas between 2008 and 2013/2018 (due to conflict-driven displacement or resampling), the unweighted estimate absorbs this compositional change. Whether the shift is genuine conflict effect or sampling artefact cannot be resolved without LGA-level longitudinal data. The sampling weights partially correct for this by reweighting toward the LGA population distribution. Until the cluster-resampling mechanism is better understood, the weighted estimate (−8.65 pp†) and education-controlled estimate (−9.4 pp*) should be treated as lower bounds with the within-state estimate (−7.2 pp†) as the preferred specification. The unweighted −16.2 pp is an upper bound. Checking whether the sampling frame (cluster allocation) changed systematically between rounds is a high-priority robustness check before submission. Instrumental Variables

Four geographic instruments—each interacted with a post-2009 indicator—are constructed as candidate instruments for LGA-level conflict intensity. Z1 (rainfall stability) uses the inverse coefficient of variation of annual CHIRPS rainfall (2005–2018); it is discarded because it exhibits a sign reversal in the second stage, consistent with weak-instrument behaviour even though it clears the conventional F threshold. Z2 (Boko Haram proximity) is the inverse distance to Maiduguri; it fails the first stage (F = 1.8) and is discarded. The two remaining instruments use long-run climate data from WorldClim v2.1. Z3 (agro-ecological transition zone) scores LGAs by their proximity to the forest–savanna transition belt (~1,200–1,800 mm annual precipitation), where farmer–herder competition is structurally elevated. Z4 (tsetse/cattle suitability) uses temperature and precipitation jointly to proxy historical tsetse-fly suitability; tsetse historically excluded cattle-keeping from humid zones, so high-suitability LGAs have less tradition of coexistence and more conflict when Fulani herders move in post-2009. Both instruments are predetermined by long-run climate; their level effects on health are absorbed by LGA fixed effects.

Exclusion restriction caveat. The main threat to validity is that ecological zone predicts health outcomes through pathways other than farmer–herder conflict. High-precipitation transition zones have higher malaria burden, denser vegetation (affecting infrastructure), and were more likely to receive certain health programme expansions (e.g., urban-edge facility investment). Agricultural productivity shocks correlated with the transition zone could independently affect utilisation. The instruments are used primarily to verify the sign of the OLS effect and check for attenuation bias; they do not fully resolve confounding from these ecological channels. The OLS DiD remains the preferred estimate.

Z4 mechanism: The tsetse fly historically prevented cattle-keeping in humid zones (Rogers & Randolph 1993). 2SLS estimates (Figure 4 below): Both Z3 and Z4 produce estimates that are consistent in sign with OLS and significant at p < 0.001 for all four outcomes. However, the magnitudes are implausibly large: Z3 implies 5–6× amplification over OLS (skilled delivery −0.216 vs. −0.036 OLS); Z4 implies 9–10× (−0.333). At these scales, the Z4 estimate alone would reduce skilled delivery from a 50% baseline to approximately 17%, which is at the outer bound of plausibility even in severely conflict-affected areas. Likely explanations include: (i) LATE amplification—compliers are the most conflict-exposed LGAs, where effects may genuinely be larger; (ii) attenuation bias in OLS from measurement error in LGA-level conflict counts; (iii) incomplete exclusion restriction—the ecological instruments may capture correlated health pathway variation. Note: Z3 and Z4 are moderately correlated (r = −0.45) and share overlapping ecological mechanisms; they are not independent. The correct critical value for weak-instrument tests with cluster-robust SEs is the Montiel Olea–Pflueger effective F ≈ 23.1 (10% size distortion); Z3 passes this, Z4 is borderline. The convergence in sign across both instruments and OLS supports the direction of the effect; the 2SLS magnitudes should be treated as upper bounds rather than point estimates.

Statistical Power & Identification Summary

Is every analysis underpowered? Not uniformly. The primary LGA+year FE model (G = 182, N = 21,517) has adequate power for effects ≥10 pp (MDE ≈ 8 pp at 80% power; SE ≈ 4 pp). The state × year FE spec is underpowered (MDE = 11.7 pp, SE = 4.2 pp; observed −7.2 pp = 47% power) because absorbing 35 SFE parameters consumes precision that was doing identification work. The mother FE design has a precise null (SE = 1.6 pp); its null is a genuine finding, not a power failure.

Why attenuation with SFE?
Not just power — also composition		 The −16.2 pp → −7.2 pp attenuation with SFE has two components: (i) compositional selection removes ~40% (education control: −9.4 pp); (ii) state-level time trends remove another ~30%. The residual −7.2 pp is the within-state, compositionally-adjusted estimate. It is underpowered, but even if fully powered, the true within-state effect is likely smaller than the raw −16.2 pp. The mother FE design — which eliminates compositional selection entirely — finds a null, suggesting the true individual-level causal effect may be small or zero. Ecological-level effects on LGA infrastructure may be real even if individual-level effects are small. INTERPRETATION

A note on the within-mother FE validity: this design exploits the DHS birth history collected within a single survey wave. Each 2013 respondent reports all her births retrospectively, so a "straddler mother" is the same woman comparing her pre-2009 birth to her post-2009 birth — no panel of respondents across waves is required. The cross-wave cluster non-overlap (only ~20% of clusters within 5km across waves) affects the DiD, not the mother FE. The mother FE is valid and the null is genuine.

Contraception is no longer the paper's strongest finding (pre-trend violation). The HQA attendant shift is no longer the paper's credible mechanism (sibling FE null for all four attendant types). The convergence trajectory and compositional confound explain the LGA DiD results.

What survives at the LGA level: Financial access barrier (+8.3pp**, v467b) has not yet been tested under sibling FE and remains the last untested mechanism. However, given the uniform null pattern across all other outcomes under within-family identification, there is strong prior reason to expect this will also attenuate.

The paper's empirically defensible contribution is documentation of a pattern consistent with compositional population change in conflict-affected areas — conflict may have altered who lives where and who is surveyed rather than changing individual healthcare-seeking behaviour within the same family. The LGA DiD estimates are upward-biased due to: (a) education compositional shift; (b) sampling non-overlap across DHS waves; (c) pre-trend divergence in the most robust outcome (contraception). Framings A–C are not supported. A null-and-reframing paper (Framing D) documenting the methodological failure modes is the most honest contribution.

Framing C (mechanisms/pathways) would require the HQA or money-barrier sibling FE to show a positive result — currently not demonstrated. Do not submit under Framing C without that evidence.
